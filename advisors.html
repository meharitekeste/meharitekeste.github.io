Director

Dr. Mehari Tekeste

Associate Professor, Department of Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering
Director, Soil Machine Dynamics Laboratory (SMDL)

Mehari Tekeste leads a research team on Physical Systems Modeling and Simulation of Agricultural and Off-Road Machinery Systems at the Department of Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering (ABE) at Iowa State University. He teaches the undergraduate course Functional Analysis of Soil, Crop and Machine Systems and the graduate course Modeling and Simulation of Soil-Crop-Machine Systems.

He has published over fifty papers and is Editor of the Journal of Terramechanics, and is actively involved in standards development and leadership roles related to soil dynamics in ISTVS and ASABE.

Read full bio

His current research focus includes (1) developing models of particle (soil & crop)-to-machine interactions, and advancing multi-physics virtual engineering analysis for off-road equipment design and machine performance analysis, and (2) advancing soil dynamics research for soil compaction, traction, and tillage precision applications.

Dr. Tekeste also has industry experience working in design and performance analysis of various Ground Engaging Tools (GET) (subsoiler, cultivator sweep, loader buckets) for agriculture and construction equipment, virtual performance analysis of large tractor tire/track design solutions for predicting traction forces and soil deformation, and modeling crop handling systems of crop harvesting equipment.

He has supervised many graduate students, postdoctoral associates and visiting scholars, and established the Soil Machine Dynamics Laboratory at ISU with three soil bins: the mobility soil bin for single-tire mobility studies, the circular wear testing bin for accelerated wear testing, and the GETs soil bin for GET design and performance analysis.

Advisory board

Dr. Thomas Way

Agricultural Engineer, USDA-ARS National Soil Dynamics Laboratory, Auburn, Alabama

Research on tractive performance and soil compaction characteristics of agricultural vehicles. Author or coauthor of over 80 peer-reviewed journal articles and an inventor on one US patent.

Read full bio

His research includes investigating effects of loads and inflation pressures of agricultural drive tires, the lug height and aspect ratio of agricultural drive tires, and comparing a mechanical front-wheel drive (MFWD) tractor with a rubber track agricultural tractor. His work includes using Stress State Transducers (SSTs) for determining stresses in soil beneath tires and rubber tracks, measuring soil-tire and soil-track interface pressures, investigating deflections of tire tread relative to the rim, and research about power hop. His research also includes a prototype field implement for shallow subsurface band application of poultry litter, and the performance of closing wheels on row crop planters.

Paul Corcoran

Retired Caterpillar engineer

Thirty-six of his thirty-eight-year career at Caterpillar were spent in earth mechanics research. He retired as manager of Caterpillar's earth mechanics lab, where soil bins were the primary research tool.

Read full bio

Paul worked in product research and development with all earthmoving tools and attachments and mobility, traction, and compaction systems, including soil and rock, landfill waste and asphalt paving materials. His product innovations include the Caterpillar belt track ag tractor and intelligent compaction. His work used both similitude (scale model) systems and field testing.

He grew up on a small farm in Nebraska, earned a master's degree in Agricultural Engineering from the University of Nebraska, did cooperative research with Iowa State University during his career, and continues to share his earth mechanics background with ISU.

Dr. Robert L. Schafer

Former Laboratory Director, USDA-ARS National Soil Dynamics Laboratory

A 35-year research career in agricultural engineering, 30 of them at the National Soil Dynamics Laboratory in Auburn, Alabama. ASABE Fellow and recipient of the Cyrus Hall McCormick–Jerome Increase Case Gold Medal.

Read full bio

Dr. Schafer earned his BS and MS in Agricultural Engineering, and his PhD in Agricultural Engineering and Engineering Mechanics (minor, Soil Physics) at Iowa State University. Many of his publications relate to scale or mathematical modeling of soil-machine systems. He co-organized the seminar series Similitude of Soil-Machine Systems, compiled into an ASABE special publication, and co-organized and co-hosted the First International Conference on Soil Dynamics.

His applied research helped improve the design of an efficient rolling cultivator, developed automatic guidance systems for agricultural machinery, and helped Bell Laboratories develop a sea plow for burying transatlantic cables. At the NSDL he served as Research Leader of the Tillage Research Unit and as Laboratory Director.

Prof. Vladimir V. Vantsevich

Professor, Department of Mechanical Engineering, University of Alabama at Birmingham

Director and PI of the Autonomous Vehicle Mobility Institute Program. Research on mechanical and intelligent mechatronic multi-physics systems, system modeling, design and control.

Read full bio

Applications include autonomous and conventional multi-wheel off-road vehicles, vehicle driveline systems and chassis. He developed inverse ground vehicle dynamics, the basis of his optimization of power distribution among the drive wheels and control of vehicle mobility, energy/fuel consumption, traction performance, maneuver and stability of motion. His recent work on autonomous vehicles covers coupled and interactive dynamics of vehicle systems, agile tire dynamics, virtual drivelines for electric and hybrid vehicles, and AI-based morphing of autonomous vehicles.
