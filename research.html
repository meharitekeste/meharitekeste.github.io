Research

Physical systems modeling and simulation of agricultural and off-road machinery systems

Our research focus includes (1) developing models of particle (soil and crop)-to-machine interactions, and advancing multi-physics virtual engineering analysis for off-road equipment design and machine performance analysis, and (2) advancing soil dynamics research for soil compaction, traction, and tillage precision applications.

Research areas

Methodology & test development

  • Develop testing methodology for high-fidelity calibration of soil and biomass materials
  • Integrate into simulation-based design
  • Technology development for off-road and agricultural machinery equipment

Based on systematic design of experiments (DOE), laboratory testing and evaluation of industrial applications.

Multi-physics modeling

Physical systems modeling of material-to-machine interactions using:

  • Finite element method (FEM)
  • Computational fluid dynamics (CFD)
  • Discrete element method (DEM)
  • Multibody dynamics (MBD)

Testing (soil–machine systems)

  • Soil-to-machine interaction (tire, blade)
  • Soil stress for performance testing
  • Data for model validation
  • Soil cutting forces
  • Tire/track forces

Data analytics for precision tillage & soil compaction

  • Measurement and prediction of soil compaction and its relationship to yield
  • Prescription tillage mapping using multisensory soil fusion

Applications

  • Soil–machine interaction: soil modeling and soil testing for tires, tracks and Ground Engaging Tools
  • Crop–machine interaction: particle models of biomass for harvesting, grain handling and crop separation equipment

How tests feed models

  • DEM models of bulldozer-blade and cultivator-sweep interaction with soil
  • Worn tools are 3D scanned and brought back into simulation
  • Tire footprints and rut measurements link contact area to traction and compaction
Projects

Research projects

From the lab's research group poster.

Development of experimental tests for DEM calibration

Calibration of micro-mechanics contact models requires physics-based bulk material tests fit for the application. The DEM methodology involves design of experiment (DOE), stochastic surrogate fitting for DEM parameters and multi-response optimization algorithms.

DEM–multibody dynamics (MBD) coupling for soil–tool interaction

Coupling DEM and MBD simulates soil-to-tool interaction to obtain accurate loads for complex materials and different machine motions.

Wear testing of soil-engaging tools

Controlled wear tests of soil-engaging tools in the circular wear testing bin, and face hardening design evaluation for wear.

Machine traffic–induced soil compaction and its impact on long-term yield

Eco-physiological response of soybean and corn to compacted soil in the root zone, and deep tillage management to alleviate deep soil compaction from construction activities.

Modeling flexible fibers using DEM

DEM bonds between specific spheres represent flexible fibers. Experiments on wheat straw fiber provide property values such as diameters, weights, lengths and mechanical properties.

DEM simulation for worn tool analysis

3D scanning and reverse engineering integrate worn tools into the simulation workflow, so the performance of tillage tools from field service (worn vs. new) can be analyzed virtually.

Crop modeling

Calibrated particle models for crop handling

Ongoing research develops new in-situ and ASTM standard testing methods for measuring flow, shear and compression loading of biomass particles (corn, soybean, rice and wheat straw), and applies design of experiment (DOE) and optimization techniques for DEM simulation of crop-engaging equipment in grain handling and in mechanical and pneumatic crop separation systems.

The DEM particle models predict discharge mass flow rates from hopper orifices (corn, rice and soybean), impact forces on combine grain yield systems for varying corn moisture content, and compression pressure of wheat straw in confined chambers with less than ten percent relative error, enabling accelerated simulation-based design of industry-scale grain harvesting systems.

Space research

From Iowa soil to lunar regolith

The same controlled testing that serves farm and construction equipment now supports NASA work on machines that dig and drive on the Moon.

NASA-funded

Tool wear in icy regolith

A study predicted wear rates for a chisel designed to harvest water from icy lunar regolith, including vacuum and temperature-swing conditions.

Test media

Lunar soil simulant

A lunar-surface-like soil developed in the lab was used to reproduce those conditions. The lab can also create other simulants on request.

Artemis

Mobility on deformable ground

Dr. Tekeste is part of a multi-institutional review of mobility studies on deformable surfaces for an autonomous vehicle tied to NASA's Artemis mission.
