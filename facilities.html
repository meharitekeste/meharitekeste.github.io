Facilities

Three types of soil testing equipment

The Soil Machine Dynamics Laboratory (SMDL) at Iowa State University, Boone, Iowa, has three types of soil testing equipment: a mobility soil bin, a circular wear testing bin, and a Ground Engaging Tools (GETs) soil bin.

01 · Single-tire tester

Mobility soil bin

The mobility soil bin is comprised of mechanical, electrical, control and measurement systems to prepare soil and conduct tire/track tests for tractive technology development, mobility, and soil–tire interaction studies. Besides technology and tire-performance tests, the data can also be used to validate physical systems modeling and algorithm development, such as tractive efficiency optimization at different tire and soil parameters.

The bin includes a soil processing unit and a testing dyno carriage. Soil processing is performed with a rotary tiller, a bucket-blade leveler, and a load-driven roller to create uniform or stratified soil layers. The bin is filled with an artificial soil mix (sand, clay, and mineral oil) to simulate the behavior of cohesive frictional soil.

02 · Wear studies of GETs

Circular wear testing bin

The circular wear testing bin is designed for wear studies of Ground Engaging Tools (GETs) and is filled with an abrasive pea-gravel medium. It has four testing arms: two for attaching tillage tools and two for attaching reconditioning blades.

The arms are powered by an electric motor with gear-ratio selection. The moisture content of the pea-gravel abrasive medium can be monitored using an automated irrigation system attached to the bin.

03 · Ground Engaging Tools

GETs soil bin

The GETs soil bin is designed for testing scaled earth-moving attachments such as buckets, blades, and other soil-cutting devices. It allows performance testing for energy efficiency and moving soils from earth-moving equipment.

The bin consists of a soil processing unit, with similar functions as in the mobility soil bin, and a dyno carriage with an instrumented tool plate for measuring displacement, force and moment of the GETs. It is powered with servo-electric systems compatible with control systems for earth-moving machinery attachments.

Off-road machine systems with soil-engaging attachments can be simulated in real time to control the tests through LabVIEW. For dozer blade testing, for example, machine drawbar and speed capabilities, track slip, machine-to-tool linkage and various control algorithms can be used. The soil reaction forces, moments and soil rupture can be scaled to actual equipment loading under field soil conditions, and various soil types and conditions can be reproduced.

Size15 m long × 1.5 m wide × 0.25 m deep
AttachmentsBulldozer blades · wheel loader buckets · rippers (subsoilers) · grader blades
